# Honey Bunny Kidz — trend plan & content calendar

Researched 2026-10-26. The daily task reads this file in STEP 3: it follows the calendar row for today's date, and when fewer than 7 future rows are left, it researches trends again and adds 2 more weeks.

## What's working for under-6s right now (and how we use it)

| Trend | Evidence | How Honey Bunny Kidz uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **Songs rule YouTube kids** | Most of YouTube's top 10 kids channels are music channels (CoComelon, Super Simple Songs, ChuChu TV, Pinkfong). | Sing-along episodes go from every 4th day to **2 per week**. Use a repeated chorus, actions (clap, hop, wiggle), counting, and routines. All songs are original. |
| **A face a 2-year-old can name** | Chart leaders are "a face first and a show second" (Bluey, Peppa, CoComelon). | Keep a **fixed core cast**: Honey, Mimi, Dotty, Lulu, Pip, Mama, Ollie and Moon. Put the star's name in every title and thumbnail. Add a new friend only about once a week. |
| **Short, replayable episodes** | Bluey runs about 7 min, Peppa about 5, rhymes about 3. Replays drive the charts. | Full episodes are **about 5 min** with a fast 45-second opening (retention fix 2026-11-09), plus 10-min mini compilations on Wed/Sat and the Sunday compilation. End on the chorus + a teaser for the next episode. |
| **Interactive "talk to the viewer" learning** (Ms Rachel style) | Ms Rachel's pause-and-answer style grew from YouTube into a Netflix show, and it is now a leading toddler format. | Every episode has at least 3 **ask-and-pause** moments: a character asks the viewer ("What color is it?", "Can you say moo?"), then waits about 2 s (`"pause": 2.0`) before answering. |
| **Family play and imagination** (Bluey style) | Bluey is the #1 streamed kids show in H1 2026. Parents watch along. | Some stories are imaginative play with Mama Bunny: forts, dress-up, rainy-day games. Include gentle humour that parents enjoy too. |
| **Calm / mindfulness / bedtime** | Yoga and calm-down videos are a growing bedtime niche. | Bedtime stories add a short breathing or stretch moment ("smell the flower, blow the candle"). |
| **Animal facts wrapped in a story** | Animal content and "animal fact stories" do well as Shorts. | About 1 per week. Use only simple, well-established facts (ducks' waterproof feathers, owls awake at night). |
| **Shorts: guessing games and riddle endings** | Riddle/question endings drive comments and replays. Daily posting and series formats help discovery. | Shorts use a **"Guess who / Guess what"** hook or question in the first 2 s, show the answer at the end, then "Watch the full story on Honey Bunny Kidz!". Keep a consistent title style. |
| **Seasonal moments** | Seasonal searches (Halloween, autumn, winter, holidays) spike every year. | Gentle seasonal episodes only: pumpkins, dress-up, falling leaves, first snow, cosy winter. **Nothing scary**: no ghosts, monsters, dark spooky settings or jump scares. |
| **Holiday-season music (Nov–Dec)** | Winter and Christmas songs dominate kids' viewing from mid-November (Little Baby Bum / Super Simple style "Jingle Bells" videos, toddler Christmas song lists); YouTube's 2026 priorities are cutting "AI slop" and raising kids' content quality (refresh 2026-11-10). | From 11-17 the calendar turns to **early winter**: first snow, mittens, warm animals, bird feeders, gift-sharing. Songs stay original (public-domain carols such as Jingle Bells, 1857, are allowed but keep them non-religious and add our own actions). Keep every episode a real story with a new setting/song. |

## Week 1 results (weekly report 2026-11-02) — apply to every new episode

Data: Buffer metrics for posts sent 2026-10-26 → 11-02 (Honey Bunny channels only).
- **Guess hook (`short`) wins on Facebook**: avg ~81 impressions per Reel vs ~53 learn-along (`short2`) and ~34 sing-along (`short3`); guess Reels also got most shares. Keep 3 hooks, but make the guess Short the strongest cut and post it first.
- **Ollie Owl / bedtime-sound guessing was the best FB story** ("Who says hoo-hoo?" 319 impressions; "Count the ribbits" 209). Pip's intro post (215) and Pumpkin Patch (171/117) next.
- **On YouTube only a full sing-along got traction**: the Pumpkin Counting Song full video (Lulu) = 209 of the week's 231 YT views. All 21 YouTube Shorts together got ~13 views. Songs + full videos are the YouTube priority.
- **Known faces > new friends**: put Ollie, Lulu or Pip in the title of most episodes; keep new characters to one per week at most.
- **Facebook reach dropped sharply from 10-29** (posts went from 100–300 impressions to 7–26). Newer posts may still be accruing, but 7 posts/day on one page may be too many — see note in the calendar.
- **Titles that worked**: a direct question with the star's name ("Who Says Hoo-Hoo?", "Count the Ribbits with Ollie Owl!"). Use this pattern for every Short title and FB first line.

## Retention fix (user, 2026-11-09) — apply to every new episode
YouTube retention on "Clap with Lulu" (5:56): ~65% left at 0:05, ~33% at 0:30, ~20% at 1:00, ~8% at the end. The slow opening (cover frame + title card + narrator set-up) loses most viewers. From ep019: **full videos are about 5 min (4:30–5:30; user 2026-11-09)** and follow the 7-beat structure in SETUP.md ("Retention rules"): cold-open hook in the first 5 s (SING = open on the chorus), problem by 0:20, funny moment by 0:45, challenge with ask-and-pause, gentle twist ~1:40, happy resolution, catchy chorus ending + teaser for the next calendar episode. No title-card scene and no cover frame on full videos. Cuter, brighter voices (see SETUP.md "Voices").

## Quality rules (YouTube's 2026 "inauthentic content" policy)
YouTube's July 2026 update demonetises **repetitive or templated** AI content, emotionally manipulative "distress then rescue" hooks, and AI personas of real people. So:
- Every episode needs a real story arc and a new setting, situation or song. Never just swap names in an old script.
- No peril, crying-for-views or distress hooks. Problems stay small and gentle (a lost bell, a shy feeling).
- Keep labelling uploads as AI-generated (`isAiGenerated: true`).
